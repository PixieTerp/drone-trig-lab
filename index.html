Meet the right triangle

The drone, the delivery box and the person make a right triangle: one corner is a perfect square corner (90°). Each side has a name. The names depend on where the angle θ (say "theta") is.

Why the dashed lines? They turn the triangle into a rectangle. The top dashed line is flat, just like the ground. So the drone looks down at the same angle θ that you look up. Mission 2 calls this the angle of depression.

Tap a side name to light it up.

SOH CAH TOA

This is how we remember the three trig functions.

SOHSine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse
CAHCosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse
TOATangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent
Case 1

I do NOT know a SIDE

You know the angle and one side. Use sin, cos or tan and multiply.

  • opposite = hypotenuse × sin(θ)
  • adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(θ)
  • opposite = adjacent × tan(θ)
Drone example (Mission 1): You stand 30 ft away (adjacent) and the angle is 52°. Height (opposite) = 30 × tan(52°) = 38.4 ft. Then add your eye height.
Case 2

I do NOT know the ANGLE

You know two sides. Use the inverse: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹.

  • θ = sin⁻¹(opposite ÷ hypotenuse)
  • θ = cos⁻¹(adjacent ÷ hypotenuse)
  • θ = tan⁻¹(opposite ÷ adjacent)

What is an inverse? It is the undo button. tan turns an angle into a number. tan⁻¹ turns that number back into the angle. In Desmos, type arcsin, arccos or arctan.

Drone example (Mission 2): The drone is 30 ft high (opposite) and the target is 40 ft away (adjacent). θ = tan⁻¹(30 ÷ 40) = 36.9°.

How to pick the right one

  1. Find the angle θ.
  2. Label the sides: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
  3. Circle the two sides you have or need. Opposite and adjacent? Use TOA (tan).
  4. Need a side? Multiply. Need the angle? Use the inverse.
